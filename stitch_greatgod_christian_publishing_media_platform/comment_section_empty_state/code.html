Theological Reflections

The Nature of Silent Devotion

By Dr. Elias Thorne • October 12, 1924

In the quiet spaces between spoken prayers lies a deeper conversation. The texts of early monastics often point not to the volume of their intercessions, but to the profound stillness that followed them. It is in this void, they argued, that true communion takes shape.

To cultivate such silence in a modern, clamorous age requires a disciplined stewardship of one's attention. It is not merely the absence of noise, but an active, expectant waiting—a readiness to receive what is whispered rather than proclaimed.
