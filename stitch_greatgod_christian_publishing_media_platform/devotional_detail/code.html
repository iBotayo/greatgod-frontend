The Faithfulness of Small Things

Part 4 of the "Everyday Stewardship" Series

auto_stories

Luke 16:10 (NIV)

"Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much."

In a world that constantly celebrates the massive, the viral, and the explosive, it is deeply counter-cultural to focus on the small. We are conditioned to believe that significance is measured in scale—that unless our impact is broad, it isn't deep. Yet, the Kingdom of God operates on an entirely different economy.

Jesus, in His teachings, frequently elevated the mundane to the sacred. A cup of cold water, a widow’s two small coins, a mustard seed—these were the currencies of His Kingdom. In Luke 16, He presents a profound principle of spiritual stewardship: faithfulness is not a switch we flip when the stakes are high; it is a muscle we build in the quiet, unseen moments of daily life.

"Faithfulness is not a switch we flip when the stakes are high; it is a muscle we build in the quiet, unseen moments of daily life."

Consider your current responsibilities. Perhaps you are managing a small budget, leading a team of two, or simply trying to maintain peace in your household. It is tempting to look past these tasks, waiting for the "real" ministry or the "big" opportunity to arrive. But Jesus warns us that if we cannot be trusted with the little we have now, we will inevitably mishandle the abundance we crave.

True stewardship begins exactly where you are. It asks: How are you managing the time you’ve been given today? Are you intentional with the resources at your disposal? Are you present with the people currently in your sphere of influence? The faithfulness of small things is the very foundation upon which God builds a life capable of carrying great weight.

Dr. Thomas Chalmers

Director of Theological Stewardship

Dr. Chalmers has spent over two decades writing on the intersection of faith, daily work, and financial stewardship. He lives in Grand Rapids with his family.
