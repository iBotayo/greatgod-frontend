The contemporary understanding of divine grace often finds itself entangled with modern notions of meritocracy. When we approach texts like John 40:1, we must carefully disentangle cultural expectations from theological reality.

True grace, in its most classical sense, resists the transactional nature of our daily interactions. It is not earned through effort, nor is it withheld due to failure. Instead, it operates on a fundamentally different economy—one of abundant gift.
