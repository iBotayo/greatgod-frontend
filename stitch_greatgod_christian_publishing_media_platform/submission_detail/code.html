The Theology of Waiting: A Meditation on Advent

By Dr. Elias Thorne · Submitted Oct 12, 2024 · 1,850 words

In the modern era, waiting is considered an anomaly, a glitch in the otherwise seamless delivery of instant gratification. Yet, within the biblical narrative, waiting is not an interruption of the spiritual life; it is the very fabric of it. From Abraham’s decades-long anticipation of a promised son to the Israelites' centuries in Egypt, the posture of God's people is fundamentally one of expectation.

As we approach the season of Advent, we are invited into this historical reality. The church calendar forces a disruption upon our hurried lives, demanding that we sit in the dark before we light the candles of Christmas morning.

The Discipline of the Desert

To understand waiting biblically is to understand the desert. It is in the barren places that reliance on God is not an option, but a necessity. The desert fathers understood this acutely, seeking out the silence of the wilderness not to escape reality, but to confront it without the numbing agents of civilization.

When we are forced to wait—for a diagnosis, for a prodigal to return, for clarity in vocation—we enter a personal desert. Our initial reaction is almost always rebellion. We demand a timeline. We seek a shortcut.
